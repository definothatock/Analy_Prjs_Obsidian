
> I liked zzz tv mode. But many found it not just mis-match of taste, but unacceptable. I wonder?

## Quick Review / Abstract

**TV mode was not hated because it had no value.**  
It was hated because a system with **real expressive strengths** was used as a **high-frequency, mandatory presentation layer** inside a game whose most immediately marketable strengths were **stylish combat, character performance, and fast audiovisual impact**. By late September 2024, HoYoverse publicly acknowledged heavy negative feedback, said TV mode had multiple launch issues, and moved future main-story presentation away from it starting with Version 1.2. ([pcgamesn.com](https://www.pcgamesn.com/zenless-zone-zero/tv-mode))

Also, to be precise: it was **not instantly erased from literally everything at once**. The big policy shift happened around **September 24–25, 2024**, when the team said subsequent storyline experiences would largely stop using TV mode as the main presentation method. ([pcgamesn.com](https://www.pcgamesn.com/zenless-zone-zero/tv-mode))

## 2. Gameplay Framework

### 2.1 Play Objective / Success State

At the local level, the player is trying to:

- navigate the Hollow representation
- complete the commission/story objective
- survive combat encounters
- advance the narrative ([gamespot.com](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/zenless-zone-zero-review-hackers-delight/1900-6418247/))

### 2.2 Failure Pressure

Failure pressure comes less from the TV layer itself and more from:

- combat failure
- wasted time
- pacing drag
- friction between expected action payoff and actual mission structure ([siliconera.com](https://www.siliconera.com/zenless-zone-zero-has-a-bad-pacing-problem/))

### 2.3 Core Loop

A simplified launch-era loop was:

**receive mission → enter TV grid → move through nodes / solve events → trigger dialogue / animation / combat → return to grid → continue until objective completion**

That loop could vary in surface form, but this stop-start alternation is central to why it became divisive. ([pcgamer.com](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/zenless-zone-zero-review/))

### 2.4 Progression Structure

The problem was not just that TV mode existed. It was that it appeared in a **high proportion of early play**, where first impressions were being formed. HoYoverse later said exactly that: early gameplay contained an excessive proportion of TV mode. ([pcgamesn.com](https://www.pcgamesn.com/zenless-zone-zero/tv-mode))

## 3. System Analysis

### 3.1 Central and Distinctive System

TV mode was one of launch ZZZ’s most distinctive systems because it did several things at once:

- represented Hollow traversal
- mediated story delivery
- controlled pacing between combat beats
- embodied the Proxy role rather than only the fighter role ([pcgamer.com](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/zenless-zone-zero-review/))

### 3.3A Function

**What does this system do?**

It appears to:

- abstract exploration so the game can depict Hollow navigation without building every traversal sequence in full 3D
- create mission variety through puzzle/event structures
- stage narrative beats, including shifting routes, pursuit, transformation, and simultaneous events
- express that the protagonists are **guides/operators**, not only combatants ([pcgamer.com](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/zenless-zone-zero-review/))

This is where **your positive reading is valid**. The system really does have affordances that pure combat rooms or plain dialogue struggle to provide. Because it is abstract, it can portray broader situational logic—map-wide change, concurrent hazards, chase structure, and stylized event transitions—cheaply and clearly. Previews specifically noted that the mode could transform itself into varied mini-structures and visual set-pieces. ([pcgamer.com](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/zenless-zone-zero-review/))

### 3.3B Structure

Its structure combines:

- abstract map traversal
- authored tile animations
- dialogue delivery
- puzzle/event scripting
- combat transitions

This means it is not a pure puzzle mode and not a pure story mode. It is a **hybrid mediation layer** between story and combat. That hybrid nature is also the source of the backlash. HoYoverse later said the intertwining of TV gameplay with combat interrupted player flow rather than supporting it. ([pcgamesn.com](https://www.pcgamesn.com/zenless-zone-zero/tv-mode))

### 3.3C Functional Role

Its apparent role within the project was:

- **narrative staging**
- **Hollow representation**
- **Proxy fantasy reinforcement**
- **pacing modulation between fights**

The problem is that the role it tried to perform is not the same as the role many players wanted it to perform. A large part of the audience came for **combat and characters first**. Chinese-language commentary on reception repeatedly frames this as a mismatch between the project’s action identity and the amount of non-combat process wrapped around it. ([bianews.com](https://www.bianews.com/news/details?id=189441))

### 3.3D Interactions

**Player influence:** route choice, some event handling, some puzzle solving.  
**System control:** mission cadence, interruptions, transitions, staging, pacing.

So the player does act, but the system remains highly authored. That is fine in principle, but it creates a burden: if the authored material is not rewarding enough, the player feels **processed through the system** rather than empowered by it. HoYoverse’s own summary of complaints—too long, sluggish, too frequent, interrupting combat—matches exactly that failure mode. ([pcgamesn.com](https://www.pcgamesn.com/zenless-zone-zero/tv-mode))

### 3.3E Legibility / Information Structure

Legibility was mixed.

**Strengths:**

- macro navigation is readable
- event tiles can communicate state cleanly
- abstract packaging makes complex situations easy to stage ([pcgamer.com](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/zenless-zone-zero-review/))

**Weaknesses:**

- too many micro-pauses, animations, and transitions dilute perceived cause-effect
- the player can understand what is happening but still feel the system is slow
- readability does not automatically mean good tempo ([pcgamesn.com](https://www.pcgamesn.com/zenless-zone-zero/tv-mode))

### 3.3F Friction / Breakdown

This is where the hatred becomes understandable.

#### 1. **Time-cost exceeded felt decision value**

The dev team itself later summarized the major complaints as: too long, sluggish animations, too much early usage, and broken combat flow. ([pcgamesn.com](https://www.pcgamesn.com/zenless-zone-zero/tv-mode))

#### 2. **It repeatedly delayed the game’s strongest pleasure**

Reviews at launch repeatedly highlight ZZZ’s combat as its major strength, while criticizing pacing and the amount of time spent away from combat. ([siliconera.com](https://www.siliconera.com/zenless-zone-zero-has-a-bad-pacing-problem/))

#### 3. **It was frontloaded into onboarding**

This is probably the biggest reason the reaction became so intense. A merely divisive system can survive if encountered later or selectively. A divisive system encountered **early, often, and mandatorily** becomes a first-impression filter. ([pcgamesn.com](https://www.pcgamesn.com/zenless-zone-zero/tv-mode))

#### 4. **It weakened character-expression payoff**

Chinese commentary made a sharp commercial/design point: in a gacha project, much of the game is structurally serving character appeal, but TV traversal reduces direct character presence and performance. ([bianews.com](https://www.bianews.com/news/details?id=189441))

#### 5. **Its variation was real, but not enough to offset its wrapper burden**

Even positive-leaning reviews noted inventive uses of the system, and previews praised the variety of events and presentations. But many players still experienced the wrapper itself as friction, not reward. ([pcgamer.com](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/zenless-zone-zero-review/))

## 4. Play Structures Analysis

### 4.1 Entry and Onboarding

TV mode appears to have failed hardest in onboarding. HoYoverse later singled out early overrepresentation, and reviewers described the launch tutorial/campaign pacing as flawed from the very start. ([pcgamesn.com](https://www.pcgamesn.com/zenless-zone-zero/tv-mode))

### 4.2 Repetition and Variation

There **was** variation: route changes, chase structures, mini-game-like puzzles, and visual transformations. But for many players, this read as **format variation inside a disliked wrapper**, not as deepening mastery. ([pcgamer.com](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/zenless-zone-zero-review/))

### 4.3 Pacing

This is the core issue.

ZZZ’s headline fantasy was high-style action. TV mode repeatedly inserted a slower, more abstract cadence between those peaks. That kind of contrast can work, but at launch it seems to have crossed from **pacing modulation** into **momentum disruption**. ([pcgamesn.com](https://www.pcgamesn.com/zenless-zone-zero/tv-mode))

### 4.4 Escalation and Mastery

The system offered more **content novelty** than **skill mastery**. Players saw new event types, but many did not feel they were becoming meaningfully better at a satisfying core practice. That makes repeated mandatory use feel longer than it is. This is an inference, but it fits both the reviews and the later developer summary of complaints. ([pcgamesn.com](https://www.pcgamesn.com/zenless-zone-zero/tv-mode))

## 5. Interpretation and Evaluation

### 5.1 System Role

**Discussion-interpreted role:**  
TV mode seems intended to be a **stylized proxy-navigation and narrative abstraction layer**. It gives form to Hollow traversal, lets the game stage unusual situations efficiently, and makes Phaethon feel like operators rather than spectators. ([pcgamer.com](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/zenless-zone-zero-review/))

It **does** fulfill that role in some missions. So your appreciation is not niche nonsense; it is identifying a real design strength.

### 5.2 Target Evaluation

The problem is audience fit.

Chinese-language commentary on reception stressed that ZZZ sat awkwardly between:

- action-game expectations
- mobile-session expectations
- HoYoverse mass-audience expectations ([bianews.com](https://www.bianews.com/news/details?id=189441))

For players primarily seeking:

- flashy combat
- character expression
- fast progression
- immediate payoff

TV mode felt like detour, not core pleasure. That mismatch becomes harsher in a live-service gacha environment, where tolerance for mandatory friction is lower than in a premium experimental title. ([bianews.com](https://www.bianews.com/news/details?id=189441))

### 5.3 Design Alignment

This is the decisive judgment:

**TV mode was not well aligned with ZZZ’s dominant perceived identity at launch.**

The game’s most legible identity was stylish, accessible action combat; even previews emphasized combat as the clearest flagship strength. The dev team later admitted TV mode reduced combat time and broke flow. That is a direct alignment problem, not just a polish problem. ([gamersky.com](https://www.gamersky.com/news/202311/1677658.shtml))

### 5.4 Communication Effectiveness

TV mode communicated **situation** well, but communicated **value** poorly.

Players could often understand what the game was showing them. But many did not feel that the time and interruption cost was justified by the decisions they were making. So the communication failure was less “I don’t know what is happening” and more “I know what is happening, but I don’t want to be doing this right now.” ([pcgamesn.com](https://www.pcgamesn.com/zenless-zone-zero/tv-mode))

### 5.5 Distinctive Strengths

TV mode’s strongest design values were:

- abstractly representing Hollow traversal
- depicting concurrent or large-scale situational change
- enabling varied event structures without full 3D staging
- reinforcing the Proxy identity of the protagonists ([pcgamer.com](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/zenless-zone-zero-review/))

### 5.6 Design Risks / Weaknesses

Its main weaknesses were:

- overuse in mandatory story flow
- weak synergy with combat-first audience expectations
- high interruption frequency
- low character-display payoff relative to a gacha project
- poor first-impression placement in onboarding ([pcgamesn.com](https://www.pcgamesn.com/zenless-zone-zero/tv-mode))

### 5.7 Constraint

A strong inference is that TV mode was also a **production-efficient abstraction**: a flexible way to depict complex Hollow situations without fully simulating every event chain in 3D space. The mode’s ability to transform into chase scenes, bombs, route puzzles, and stylized map events supports that inference. ([pcgamer.com](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/zenless-zone-zero-review/))

That is smart design economy. But smart economy is not automatically popular play. In ZZZ, the abstraction likely saved expressive cost while increasing perceived friction.

### 5.8 Overall Effectiveness

My judgment:

**TV mode failed less as a concept than as a default, mandatory, frontloaded delivery structure.**  
Used selectively, it had real strengths. Used as a frequent early wrapper around the game’s strongest content, it became a drag on momentum and audience fit. HoYoverse’s September 2024 response strongly suggests they came to the same conclusion. ([pcgamesn.com](https://www.pcgamesn.com/zenless-zone-zero/tv-mode))

## 7. Outcome Summary

### Project / System Identity

TV mode tried to make ZZZ more than “combat rooms plus dialogue.” It was a **proxy-exploration abstraction system** that could express Hollow logic, shifting situations, and stylized narrative beats. ([pcgamer.com](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/zenless-zone-zero-review/))

### Why many players hated it

Most likely because it:

1. **took too long** for the value it gave,
2. **interrupted the combat flow**,
3. was **too prominent in the early game**,
4. reduced **character/combat payoff** in a combat-gacha product, and
5. created a mismatch between **marketed identity** and **actual moment-to-moment play**. ([pcgamesn.com](https://www.pcgamesn.com/zenless-zone-zero/tv-mode))

### Why you liked it

Because its **best affordance was real**: it could stage situations, simultaneity, and abstract traversal in a way that plain dialogue or small 3D combat arenas often cannot. That is a genuine strength, not nostalgia distortion. ([pcgamer.com](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/zenless-zone-zero-review/))

### Final Judgment

So the answer is not:

> “players hated it because it was simply bad.”

It is closer to:

> **players hated it because it was a structurally interesting system placed in the wrong proportion, at the wrong time, for the wrong dominant audience expectation.**

And once ZZZ’s public identity solidified around **fast stylish combat**, TV mode became the easiest thing for the audience to blame for every feeling of slowness or friction. ([pcgamesn.com](https://www.pcgamesn.com/zenless-zone-zero/tv-mode))
